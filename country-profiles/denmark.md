# Denmark transfer pricing rules

Transfer pricing rules in Denmark, summarised from the OECD Transfer Pricing Country Profile for Denmark (updated May 2025). The profile is the questionnaire the Denmark tax administration completed for the OECD, covering the arm's length principle, transfer pricing methods, comparability, intangibles, intra-group services, financial transactions, documentation, dispute resolution and safe harbours. This page holds all 44 answered questions in plain markdown.

## The Arm's Length Principle

### Q1. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework make reference to the arm's length principle?

**Yes.**

Sources: [The arm's length principle is governed by the Tax Assessment Act (Ligningsloven), section 2](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2021/1735); [Guidance on transfer pricing is provided in the Legal Guidance (DJV), section C.D.11. (Transfer Pricing)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2049948&chk=219882)

### Q2. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework give the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines any role or status?

**Yes.**

Section 2 of the Tax Assessment Act contains the Danish arm's length provision and in the explanatory memoranda (preparatory legislative work) there is a direct reference to the arm's length principle contained and described in the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (hereafter TPG). The reference is dynamic in the sense that the most recent version of the TPG is applied in Denmark. This understanding has been verified by Danish courts.

Transfer pricing rules, guidelines and practice/case law in Denmark are described in The Legal Guidance (DJV). DJV is based on and fully consistent with the guidance provided in the TPG.

Sources: [Guidance on the role of the OECD TPG in the application of the arm's principle is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.2.1.1 (Armslængde-princippet og Transfer Pricing Guidelines)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2049960&chk=219882)

### Q3. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for a definition of related parties applicable for transfer pricing purposes?

**Yes.**

According to the Tax Assessment Act, section 2, and the Tax Control Act, section 37, control/related parties are defined based on either formal control (>50% shareholding) or actual control (e.g. control based on shareholder agreement).

Sources: A common definition of related parties is provided in both the [Tax Assessment Act (Ligningsloven), section 2, and in the](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2021/1735) [Tax Control Act (Skattekontrolloven), section 37](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2017/1535); [Guidance on the definition of related parties is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.1.2 (Hvem er omfattet af armslængdeprincippet)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2049951&chk=219882)

## Transfer Pricing Methods

### Q4. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for transfer pricing methods to be used in respect of transactions between related parties?

**Yes.**

Methods provided for: CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, TNMM, Profit Split and Other.

The approved OECD-methods are to be used in Denmark.

However, taxpayers are allowed to use other methods under the guidance in TPG 2.9. but the burden of proof is then on the taxpayer to demonstrate that the other method is more appropriate than the approved OECD-methods for pricing the transaction in question.

As also provided for in the TPG, Denmark uses various valuation techniques for determining the arm's length price/value of businesses and (intangible) assets.

Sources: [Guidance on transfer pricing methods is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.4 (Transfer pricing-metoder)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2049993&chk=219882)

### Q5. Which criterion is provided for in your domestic transfer pricing framework for the application of transfer pricing methods?

Most appropriate method.

The explanatory memoranda to the Tax Assessment Act, section 2, refers to the arm's length principle as contained and described in the latest version of the TPG.

The criterion used in Denmark for the application of transfer pricing methods is fully aligned with the guidance outlined in Chapter II of the TPG.

### Q6. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain specific guidance on commodity transactions?

**Yes.**

For controlled transactions involving commodities, the guidance contained in paragraphs 2.18-2.22 of the TPG is followed. Domestic transfer pricing framework provides for the use of a specific method for controlled transactions involving commodities.

Special tax rules govern activities related to the extraction of oil and gas – the Hydrocarbons Tax Act. Under these rules, controlled transactions involving oil and gas are priced using a CUP equivalent method.

Sources: [Hydrocarbons Tax Act (Kulbrinteskatteloven)](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2021/1820)

## Comparability Analysis

### Q7. Does your jurisdiction follow the guidance on comparability analysis outlined in Chapter III of the TPG?

**Yes.**

The explanatory memoranda to the Tax Assessment Act, section 2, refers to the arm's length principle as contained and described in the TPG.

The guidance and standard for performing a comparability analysis in Denmark is fully aligned with the guidance outlined in Chapter III of the TPG.

Sources: [Guidance on comparability analysis is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.5 (Sammenlignelighedsanalyse)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2060556&chk=219882)

### Q8. Is there a preference in your jurisdiction for domestic comparables over foreign comparables?

**No.**

There is a preference for comparable data including data from transactions on comparable markets.

Whether or not a geographic market is sufficiently comparable is determined based on the transactions in question, characteristics of the market and availability of comparable data.

### Q9. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework permit the use of secret comparables for transfer pricing assessment purposes?

**No.**

Secret comparables have not been used for transfer pricing assessment purposes.

### Q10. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow or require the use of an arm's length range and/or statistical measure for determining arm's length remuneration?

**Yes.**

Use of range and statistical tools are applied in the pricing of controlled transactions, where the use of such measures is considered appropriate, cf. the TPG.

Sources: [Guidance on use of arm's length range and/or statistical measures is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.5.9 (Tolkning og anvendelse af data til at fastlægge armslængdepriser og vilkår)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232434&chk=219882)

### Q11. Are comparability adjustments required under your domestic transfer pricing framework?

**Yes.**

Comparability adjustments are required under the circumstances described in the TPG.

Sources: [Guidance on comparability adjustments is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.5.8 (Sammenlignelighedsjusteringer)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232433&chk=219882)

## Intangible Property

### Q12. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain guidance specific to the pricing of controlled transactions involving intangibles?

**Yes.**

The framework follows (or largely follows) the guidance in Chapter VI of the TPG.

Guidance is provided on the valuation of controlled transactions involving intangibles, which is considered to be fully consistent with the guidance in the TPG on the use of valuation techniques.

Sources: [Guidance on valuation techniques to be used in controlled transactions involving intangibles is provided in DJV, section C.J. (Værdiansættelse)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=68675&chk=219882); [Guidance on application of the arm's length principle to transactions involving intangibles is also provided in DJV, section C.D.11.6. (Armslængdeprincippet i relation til immaterielle aktiver)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2060557&chk=219882)

### Q13. Are there any other rules outside your transfer pricing framework that are relevant for the pricing of controlled transactions involving intangibles?

**Yes.**

Capital gains or losses from the disposal of goodwill, know-how, patents, copyrights, designs or models, trademarks and similar rights are included in the calculation of taxable income, cf. the Tax Depreciation Act (afskrivningsloven), section 40, paragraph 6 in conjunction with paragraphs 1 and 2, and/or the State Tax Act (Statsskatteloven), section 4.

Tax Depreciation Act, section 40, paragraph 1, 2 and 6 (Non-official translation), states that:

“Paragraph 1. When acquiring goodwill, the acquirer may, with effect from the income year in which a final transfer agreement has been concluded, depreciate the purchase price when calculating the taxable income by up to 1/7 annually.

Paragraph 2. When acquiring other intangible assets such as special manufacturing methods or similar (know-how), patent law, copyright and art rights and rights to designs or trademarks… the acquirer may, with effect from the income year in which a final transfer agreement has been concluded, depreciate the purchase price when calculating the taxable income by up to 1/7 annually...

Paragraph 6. Gains and losses from sale or disposal of assets covered by paragraph 1 and 2 are included when calculating the taxable income. Gains or losses are calculated as the difference between the sales price and the acquisition price less depreciations… ”

Moreover, the State Tax Act, section 4 (Non-official translation), reads as follows:

“Paragraph 1. Taxable income, with the following exceptions and limitations, is considered to be the total annual income of the taxpayer, whether from this country or not, consisting of money or property of monetary value, such as… ”

Sources: [Tax Depreciation Act (Afskrivningsloven), section 40](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2021/242); [State Tax Act (Statsskatteloven), section 4](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/1922/149)

## Hard-to-Value Intangibles

### Q14. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain guidance specific to hard-to-value intangibles (HTVI)?

**Yes.**

The framework follows the guidance on HTVI in Chapter VI of the TPG.

Guidance on the arm's length principle – including pricing of HTVI – is applicable in Denmark, cf. the Tax Assessment Act, section 2, which contains a direct reference to the TPG in the explanatory memoranda (preparatory legislative work).

Sources: [Guidance on the arm's length principle in relation to hard to value intangibles is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.6.6 (Immaterielle aktiver, der er svære at værdiansætte)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232411&chk=219882)

### Q15. If your jurisdiction applies the HTVI approach, what are the conditions for the application of the HTVI approach?

The conditions for applying the HTVI approach in Denmark are those described in TPG 6.186 – 6.195.

The HTVI guidelines target the abuse of information asymmetry by taxpayers.

Abuse means that relevant information about the value of the intangibles has been withheld from the tax administration in order to reduce the overall tax burden of the MNE group. Therefore, the application of the HTVI rules can generally only have the effect of increasing the overall taxation of the MNE group.

### Q16. Are transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach subject to a transfer pricing analysis different from the one established in Chapters I and VI?

In Denmark, the transfer pricing analysis for transactions falling within scope of the HTVI approach is guided by TPG 6.186 – 6.195.

The analysis of HTVI transactions does not differ from the analysis established in Chapters I and VI of the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. Depending on the facts and circumstances, HTVI transfers may be subject to the provisions of the GAAR in Section 3 of the Danish Tax Assessment Act, according to which “...arrangement or a series of arrangements which, having been put into place for the main purpose or one of the main purposes of obtaining a tax advantage that defeats the object or purpose of the applicable tax law, are not genuine having regard to all relevant facts and circumstances...” can be disregarded.

Sources: [Section 3 of the Danish Tax Assessment Act, which has been implemented according to COUNCIL DIRECTIVE (EU) 2016/1164 of 12 July 2016](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2019/806#P3)

### Q17. What is the statute of limitations applicable to transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach in your domestic transfer pricing framework?

The statute of limitations for HTVI transactions is the same as for controlled transactions in general (6 years).

The period is extended to 10 years where information has been withheld from the tax authorities intentionally or through gross negligence.

Sources: [The statute of limitations for transfer pricing adjustments expires in the 6th year after the end of the income year. See section 26(5) of the Tax Administration Act (skatteforvaltningsloven), cf. subsections (1) and (2)](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2022/835#P26); [Further guidance is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.14 (Ansættelsesfrist for transfer pricing-sager)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232557&chk=219882)

### Q18. Can taxpayers request a bilateral or multilateral advance pricing agreement for transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach?

**Yes.**

Bilateral or multilateral APAs can be requested under the general terms and conditions in Danish double tax treaties.

### Q19. What measures exist or approaches have been adopted to avoid the use of hindsight?

The use of hindsight for purposes of pricing controlled transactions in Denmark is governed by the guidance provided in the TPG.

Transfer pricing staff in the Danish tax administration are highly qualified professionals. Internal instructions, training and knowledge sharing are provided and supported on an ongoing basis.

Inappropriate use of hindsight in audits would be detected before issuance of a transfer pricing assessment. TP audits are reviewed and discussed prior to completion.

### Q20. Is it possible for your tax administration to make adjustments under the HTVI approach in open years amounts pertaining to closed years?

**No.**

There is no special statute of limitations for HTVI in Denmark.

### Q21. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow the tax administration to make corresponding adjustments under the HTVI approach in open years for amounts pertaining to closed years?

**No.**

Corresponding adjustments can only be allowed for closed years if the Danish competent authority agrees to the primary adjustment.

See below under “Other Legislative Aspects or Administrative Procedures”

### Q22. Is it possible for your tax administration to make several adjustments for one single HTVI transaction under the HTVI approach?

**Yes.**

This would not be a typical scenario, but multiple adjustments are possible under the guidance in Chapters I and VI of the TPG.

**Yes.**

It follows (or largely follows) the low value-adding services approach in Chapter VII of the TPG.

Sources: [Guidance on the arm's length principle in relation to the simplified approach for low value-adding intra-group services is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.8.4 (Særligt om interne serviceydelser med lav værdi)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232463&chk=219882)

### Q25. Are there any other rules outside your transfer pricing framework for pricing intragroup services?

**Yes.**

Tax deductibility of service payments is determined under the conditions provided in the State Tax Act, section 6.

Costs related to stewardship/shareholder interests would typically not be tax deductible in Denmark.

Sources: [Tax deductibility of costs is governed by the State Tax Act (Statsskatteloven), section 6](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/1922/149)

## Transfer Pricing Documentation

### Q29. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework require the taxpayer to prepare transfer pricing documentation?

**Yes.**

Documentation required: master file consistent with Annex I to Chapter V of the TPG; local file consistent with Annex II to Chapter V of the TPG; country-by-country report consistent with Annex III to Chapter V of the TPG; specific transfer pricing returns (separate or annexed to the tax return).

Sources: [The Tax Control Act (Skattekontrolloven), section 37-52, requires taxpayers to a) disclose certain information about transfer pricing in their tax returns and b) prepare and submit transfer pricing documentation (master and local file) and c) prepare and submit CbC reports](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2017/1535); The specific transfer pricing documentation requirements in Denmark are described in [administrative ordinance on the documentation of the pricing of controlled transactions (Bekendtgørelse nr 468 af 19/04/2022 om dokumentation af prisfastsættelsen af kontrollerede transaktioner)](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2022/468) and [administrative ordinance on country by country reporting (Bekendtgørelse nr 1304 af 14/11/2018 om land for land-rapportering)](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2018/1304); [Further guidance on transfer pricing documentation requirements is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.13](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232478&chk=219882); The Danish transfer pricing documentation requirements are fully aligned with OECD standards as described in TPG chapter V and Annex I-IV to chapter V

### Q30. Please briefly explain the relevant requirements related to each transfer pricing documentation requirement.

Danish documentation requirements are provided in an administrative ordinance on documentation for the pricing of controlled transactions.

The Danish documentation requirements are aligned with the OECD three-tier approach and can be submitted in Danish, Norwegian, Swedish or English language. Submission of transfer pricing documentation is mandatory for fiscal years starting on or after 1 January 2021.

The master and local file must be submitted within 60 days after the deadline for filing the corporate income tax return.

The Country-by-Country report must be submitted no later than 12 months after the last day of the fiscal year reported for. In a sheet annexed to the tax return, companies must provide standardized information on the nature and extent of their commercial or economic transactions with related parties.

Sources: Filing of master and local file is governed by the [Tax Control Act](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2017/1535), section 37-42, and an [administrative ordinance on documentation for the pricing of controlled transactions (Bekendtgørelse nr 468 af 19/04/2022 om dokumentation af prisfastsættelsen af kontrollerede transaktioner)](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2022/468); Filing of country by country reports is governed by the [Tax Control Act](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2017/1535) (Skattekontrolloven), section 47-52, and [administrative ordinance on country by country reporting (Bekendtgørelse nr 1304 af 14/11/2018 om land for land-rapportering)](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2018/1304)

### Q31. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for specific transfer pricing penalties and/or compliance incentives?

**Yes.**

The penalties for failure to comply with the submission deadline and/or transfer pricing documentation requirements can amount to Danish Krone (DKK) 250 000 (~EUR 33 600) per company per year, plus an additional amount equal to 10% of any income adjustments issued related to non-compliance with the arm's length principle for the fiscal year.

Furthermore, the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer in transfer pricing cases where the required transfer pricing documentation has not been submitted before the deadline.

Sources: [Tax Control Act (Skattekontrolloven), section 84](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2017/1535); [Further guidance on penalties and sanctions is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.13.1.3.3. (Sanktioner ved manglende eller mangelfuld transfer pricing-dokumentation)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232789&chk=219882)

### Q32. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for exemption from transfer pricing documentation obligations?

**Yes.**

Small taxpayers, that measured at a group level employ less than 250 employees and either have revenue below DKK 250 million or a balance sheet amount below DKK 125 million, are subject to more limited documentation requirements.

Under the limited documentation requirements, small taxpayers are only required to provide transfer pricing documentation for controlled transactions with related parties in jurisdictions with which Denmark has no double tax treaty and which are not members of the EU or EEA.

Purely domestic transactions, controlled transactions between related parties both resident in Denmark and both taxed under the same tax regime, are exempt from the transfer pricing documentation requirements.

One-off transactions of insignificant financial value are partially exempted from the documentation requirement. For such transactions, only the type of transaction needs to be identified in the transfer pricing documentation.

However, all controlled transactions - including those that are exempt from the transfer pricing documentation requirements, see above - must still be carried out in accordance with the arm's length principle, and a taxpayer may be asked in connection with a tax audit to provide documentation on the arm's length pricing of all its controlled transactions.

Sources: [Tax Control Act (Skattekontrolloven), section 40](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2017/1535); [Further guidance on limited documentation requirements for small companies is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.13.1.4. (Begrænset dokumentationspligtig (mindre virksomheder) efter SKL § 40)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232791&chk=219882)

## Dispute Avoidance and Resolution

### Q33. Which mechanisms are available in your jurisdiction to prevent and/or resolve transfer pricing disputes?

Available mechanisms: Rulings; Enhanced engagement or cooperative compliance programmes; Advance Pricing Agreements (APA) (Unilateral APAs, Bilateral APAs, Multilateral APAs); International Compliance Assurance Programme (ICAP); Mutual Agreement Procedures.

Rulings: Taxpayers can apply for binding rulings in Denmark on tax related questions, and in principle also questions on transfer pricing. In practice, however, binding rulings are only rarely provided on issues related to transfer pricing.

Enhanced relationship programs: Select taxpayers have access to enhanced relationship programs where the reasonableness or risk of applied transfer prices or TP set-ups could be discussed with the Danish tax authorities. Such informal discussions would not have any binding effect on either party.

APA and MAP: Information on access to MAP and APA is provided in the Danish OECD dispute resolution profile.

Sources: [Denmark's MAP Profile](https://www.oecd.org/tax/dispute/denmark-dispute-resolution-profile.pdf)

### Q34. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow the application of the simplified and streamlined approach for baseline marketing and distribution activities?

In line with the Inclusive Framework political commitment, Denmark currently only allows for the application of the simplified and streamlined approach for baseline marketing and distribution activities in respect of transactions with wholesale distributors resident in “covered jurisdictions”, cf. the OECD Statement, provided that Denmark has a double tax treaty with the relevant jurisdiction.

Sources: As of January 2025, the legislative framework for implementing the political commitment is in the process of being drafted and adopted

### Q37. Does your jurisdiction respect the outcome of the application of the simplified and streamlined approach by a covered jurisdiction?

**Yes.**

See response to question 34 above.

## Other Legislative Aspects

### Q40. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow downward corresponding adjustments in the absence of a mutual agreement procedure?

**Yes.**

A downward transfer pricing adjustment in Denmark is premised on a corresponding upward adjustment in another jurisdictions, cf. the Tax Assessment Act (Ligningsloven), section 2, paragraph 6.

Downward corresponding adjustments can be allowed without a mutual agreement procedure (e.g. as a unilateral corresponding adjustment) if sufficient documentation for the foreign upward transfer pricing adjustment has been provided and the Danish Tax Agency approves of the foreign tax authority's decision.

Taxpayers may apply for an extraordinary reassessment under Section 27(1)(4) of the Tax Administration Act. This provision ensures that a taxpayer can obtain a corresponding adjustment after the normal time limit has expired, if the request for adjustment is a direct result of the foreign tax authority's decision. The conditions for extraordinary reassessment are that the Danish Tax Agency approves the foreign tax authority's decision, that the request is made within six months of the foreign tax authority's decision, and that the derivative claim arising from the corresponding adjustment has not lapsed after the 10-year time limit in Section 34a(4) of the Tax Administration Act.

Sources: The [Danish Tax Administration Act](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2022/835) [(Skatteforvaltningsloven)](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2024/1053#P27), section 27(1)(4); [Further guidance on statutes of limitations is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.14 (Ansættelsesfrist for transfer pricing-sager)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232557&chk=219882)

### Q41. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow or require taxpayers to make year-end adjustments?

**Yes.**

Year-end adjustments are allowed.

Year-end adjustments are allowed in Denmark to the extent that such adjustments are considered in accordance with the guidance provided in Chapter III of the TPG.

### Q42. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for secondary adjustments?

**Yes.**

Secondary adjustments are made to the extent that such adjustments have tax consequences - withholding tax, taxable dividends, etc.

Sources: [Guidance on secondary adjustments is provided in DJV, section C.D.11.16 (Sekundære justeringer og betalingskorrektion)](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=2232625&chk=219882)

## Attribution of Profits to Permanent Establishments

### Q43. Which version of Article 7 of the OECD Model Tax Convention on Income and on Capital do your tax treaties contain?

Article 7 as it read before 2010: Please see the response below. Article 7 as it reads after 2010: Please see the response below.

The wording of article 7 in most of Denmark's actual double tax treaties (about 80) corresponds to the pre-2010 version of Article 7 in the OECD Model Tax Convention. Only two of the double tax treaties are based on the post-2010 version of Article 7.

### Q44. For tax treaties containing Article 7 as it read before 2010, does your jurisdiction apply the authorized OECD approach?

**Yes.**

Where the wording of Article 7 in a tax treaty corresponds to the pre-2010 version of Article 7 in OECD's Model Tax Convention, the AOA from 2008 will be applied, cf. the comments to article 7 of the OECD MTC 2008, and the 2008 Report on the attribution of profits to permanent establishments.

### Q45. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain specific guidance for the attribution of profits to permanent establishments of non resident entities?

**Yes.**

They follow the AOA as described in the 2008 Report on the Attribution of Profits to Permanent Establishments. They follow the AOA as described in the 2010 Report on the Attribution of Profits to Permanent Establishments.
