# Belgium transfer pricing rules

Transfer pricing rules in Belgium, summarised from the OECD Transfer Pricing Country Profile for Belgium (updated July 2025). The profile is the questionnaire the Belgium tax administration completed for the OECD, covering the arm's length principle, transfer pricing methods, comparability, intangibles, intra-group services, financial transactions, documentation, dispute resolution and safe harbours. This page holds all 47 answered questions in plain markdown.

## The Arm's Length Principle

### Q1. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework make reference to the arm's length principle?

**Yes.**

Sources: [Art. 185, §2, BITC 92 – explicit reference to the ALP Art. 26, 54, 79 and 206/3 BITC 92 – implicit reference to the ALP – use of the term "abnormal or benevolent" advantages Art. 344 BITC 92 – implicit reference to the ALP – use of the term "legitimate need of a financial or economic nature"](http://www.dekamer.be/FLWB/pdf/51/1079/51K1079001.pdf)

### Q2. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework give the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines any role or status?

**Yes.**

The OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines can be used as a source of interpretation for the application of the aforementioned articles. With regards to the ambulatory application of the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, newer versions of the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines can be used with respect to transactions that occur prior to its publication, insofar that they provide clarifications of earlier versions. When the new versions contain aspects/concepts that are considered novel, they can only applied to transactions that occur after the date of publication of the new version. There is no overview of what is considered as a clarification, or as a novelty in newer versions.

Sources: [Parliamentary memorandum accompanying the Law of June 21st 2004 introducing art. 185, § 2 BITC](http://www.dekamer.be/FLWB/pdf/51/1079/51K1079001.pdf); [Circular Letter n° AFZ/98-003 of 28 June 1999](https://www.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/public/fisconet/document/8c174699-7ce1-45ca-b787-8d8c0de7ad4d); [Circular Letter n° 2020/C/35 of 25 February 2020](https://www.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/public/fisconet/document/25aa2105-24d3-4b13-89dd-2f9ba7db7e63)

### Q10. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow or require the use of an arm's length range and/or statistical measure for determining arm's length remuneration?

**Yes.**

The Belgian administration follows the guidance of the OECD TPG and allows for the use of an arm's length range. Only in exceptional circumstances (when all comparables have a very high degree of comparability), the use of the full range will be accepted. If those exceptional circumstances are not met, the Belgian administration will require the use of the interquartile range. When the transfer price falls outside the (interquartile) range that has been accepted, the Belgian administration will generally correct towards the median of the accepted (interquartile) range.

Sources: [§§ 123 – 126 of the Circular Letter n° 2020/C/35 of 25 February 2020](https://www.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/public/fisconet/document/25aa2105-24d3-4b13-89dd-2f9ba7db7e63)

### Q11. Are comparability adjustments required under your domestic transfer pricing framework?

**Yes.**

Belgium allows for comparability adjustments when these improve comparability.

Sources: [§§ 118 – 122 of the Circular Letter n° 2020/C/35 of 25 February 2020](https://www.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/public/fisconet/document/25aa2105-24d3-4b13-89dd-2f9ba7db7e63)

## Hard-to-Value Intangibles

### Q14. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain guidance specific to hard-to-value intangibles (HTVI)?

**Yes.**

3 The Circular letter n° 2020/C/35 of 25 February 2020 allows the Belgian tax administration to apply the guidance contained in §§ 6.186 – 6.194 of the OECD TPG.

Follows TPG Chapter VI (HTVI)

Sources: [§§ 153 – 163 of the Circular Letter n° 2020/C/35 of 25 February 2020](https://www.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/public/fisconet/document/25aa2105-24d3-4b13-89dd-2f9ba7db7e63)

### Q15. If your jurisdiction applies the HTVI approach, what are the conditions for the application of the HTVI approach?

**N/A.**

Circular letter n° 2020/C/35 of 25 February 2020 defines a HTVI as an intangible for which (i) no reliable comparables exists, and (ii) at the time of the transaction was entered into, the projections of future cash flows or the assumptions used in valuing the intangible are highly uncertain (cf. § 6.189 OECD TPG). Furthermore, the limitations contained in § 6.193 of the OECD TPG apply.

Sources: [§§ 153 – 163 of the Circular Letter n° 2020/C/35 of 25 February 2020](https://www.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/public/fisconet/document/25aa2105-24d3-4b13-89dd-2f9ba7db7e63)

### Q17. What is the statute of limitations applicable to transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach in your domestic transfer pricing framework?

**N/A.**

The statute of limitations for HTVI is the same as for the general transfer pricing audits, namely 6 years (10 years in the case of tax fraud).

### Q18. Can taxpayers request a bilateral or multilateral advance pricing agreement for transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach?

**Yes.**

### Q19. What measures exist or approaches have been adopted to avoid the use of hindsight?

**N/A.**

Circular letter n° 2020/C/35 of 25 February 2020 describes the HTVI approach. Further, regular trainings are provided for tax auditors in order to avoid the use of hindsight.

### Q20. Is it possible for your tax administration to make adjustments under the HTVI approach in open years amounts pertaining to closed years?

### Q21. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow the tax administration to make corresponding adjustments under the HTVI approach in open years for amounts pertaining to closed years?

**Yes.**

### Q22. Is it possible for your tax administration to make several adjustments for one single HTVI transaction under the HTVI approach?

### Q27. Are there any other rules outside your transfer pricing framework that are relevant for the tax treatment of financial transactions?

**Yes.**

Belgium has several rules that limit interest deductions: - Non-deductibility of total interests paid to an associated entity which is located in a low-taxed jurisdiction; - Non-deductibility of exceeding interests paid that are higher than interest paid under arm's length conditions; - Non-deductibility of exceeding interests paid to an entity located in a low- taxed jurisdiction that has a thin cap ratio of 5:1 or higher; - Non-deductibility of the exceeding borrowing costs paid to an associated enterprise that exceed 30% of its EBITDA or 3 million EUR (cf. BEPS Action 4 & EU Directive 2016/1164).

Sources: [See respectively: - Art. 54 BITC 92 - Art. 55 BITC 92 - Art. 198, §1, 11° BITC 92 - Art. 198/1 BITC 92](http://www.dekamer.be/FLWB/pdf/51/1079/51K1079001.pdf)

### Q30. Please briefly explain the relevant requirements related to each transfer pricing documentation requirement.

**N/A.**

The Country-by-Country Reporting Framework follows the guidance of the OECD TPG. Reporting entities: The Master File & Local File need to be filed by Belgian group entities that are part of a MNE that has: - An operational turnover of more than 50 million EUR; or - A balance sheet total of more than 1 billion EUR; or - A staff headcount of more than 100 FTE. Timing: The Master File has to be introduced at the latest 12 months after the end of the fiscal year of the MNE group. The Local File has to be introduced together with the annual tax return of the Belgian entity. Accepted languages: All returns may be completed in English, French, Dutch or German. Other elements: The Local File contains an additional return that has to be completed when a relevant business unit has intra-group transactions with a value of more than 1 million EUR.

Sources: [Art. 321/1 – 321/7 BITC 92](http://www.dekamer.be/FLWB/pdf/51/1079/51K1079001.pdf); [Circular Letter 2017/C/56 from 4 September 2017](https://www.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/public/fisconet/document/8c174699-7ce1-45ca-b787-8d8c0de7ad4d); [Circular Letter 2019/C/14 from 8 February 2019 BEPS Action 13 website](https://www.minfin.fgov.be/myminfin-web/pages/public/fisconet/document/8c174699-7ce1-45ca-b787-8d8c0de7ad4d)
