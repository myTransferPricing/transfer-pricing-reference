# New Zealand transfer pricing rules

Transfer pricing rules in New Zealand, summarised from the OECD Transfer Pricing Country Profile for New Zealand (updated July 2025). The profile is the questionnaire the New Zealand tax administration completed for the OECD, covering the arm's length principle, transfer pricing methods, comparability, intangibles, intra-group services, financial transactions, documentation, dispute resolution and safe harbours. This page holds all 44 answered questions in plain markdown.

## The Arm's Length Principle

### Q1. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework make reference to the arm's length principle?

**Yes.**

Sources: [Sections YD 5, YD 5B, GB 2 and GC 6-14 of the Income Tax Act 2007](http://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0097/latest/DLM1512301.html)

### Q2. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework give the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines any role or status?

**Yes.**

New Zealand's domestic transfer pricing legislation explicitly refers to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines 20 22 and requires domestic rules apply consistently with these.

Sources: [Section GC 6 (1B) of the Income Tax Act 2007](http://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0097/latest/DLM1512301.html)

### Q3. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for a definition of related parties applicable for transfer pricing purposes?

**Yes.**

New Zealand's transfer pricing rules apply to cross-border arrangements between associated persons, based on 50% or greater common shareholding or effective control. Section GB 2 can extend the application of sections GC 7-10 to non-associated parties where there is a collateral arrangement (such as a market-sharing arrangement, an arrangement to enter into a particular market, a back-to-back supply arrangement or an income-sharing arrangement).

Sources: [Subparts YA and YB of the Income Tax Act 2007, in particular section YB 2(1)](http://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0097/latest/DLM1512301.html)

## Transfer Pricing Methods

### Q4. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for transfer pricing methods to be used in respect of transactions between related parties?

**Yes.**

Methods provided for: CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, TNMM, Profit Split.

Sources: [Section GC 13(2) of the Income Tax Act 2007](http://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0097/latest/DLM1512301.html)

### Q5. Which criterion is provided for in your domestic transfer pricing framework for the application of transfer pricing methods?

Most appropriate method.

Under section GC 1 3(1), the method that produces the most reliable measure of the arm's length amount of consideration must be applied. Section GC 13(3) provides criteria for the choice and application of the method. New Zealand follows the detailed guidance in Chapter III of the OECD TPG.

Sources: [Sections GC 13(1) to (3) of the Income Tax Act 2007](http://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0097/latest/DLM1512301.html)

### Q6. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain specific guidance on commodity transactions?

**Yes.**

For controlled transactions involving commodities, the guidance contained in paragraphs 2.18-2.22 of the TPG is followed.

New Zealand's domestic transfer pricing legislation explicitly refers to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines 20 22 and requires domestic rules apply consistently with these.

## Comparability Analysis

### Q7. Does your jurisdiction follow the guidance on comparability analysis outlined in Chapter III of the TPG?

**Yes.**

Under section GC 1 3(3), the choice and application of a transfer pricing method must be made having regard to the degree of comparability between the uncontrolled transactions used for comparison and the controlled transactions of the taxpayer.

New Zealand's domestic transfer pricing legislation explicitly refers to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines 20 22 and requires domestic rules apply consistently with these.

Sources: [Section GC 13(3) of the Income Tax Act 2007](http://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0097/latest/DLM1512301.html)

### Q8. Is there a preference in your jurisdiction for domestic comparables over foreign comparables?

**No.**

Appropriately selected overseas data is accepted where the source market is sufficiently comparable. Australia is generally recognised as a sufficiently comparable market.

Sources: [Comparability analysis](https://www.ird.govt.nz/international-tax/business/transfer-pricing/practice-issues/comparability-analysis)

### Q9. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework permit the use of secret comparables for transfer pricing assessment purposes?

**Yes.**

It is legally possible, the domestic legislation provides for the use of secret comparables, but in practice, secret comparables are not used.

### Q10. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow or require the use of an arm's length range and/or statistical measure for determining arm's length remuneration?

**Yes.**

Rather than requiring the use of an arm's length range and/or statistical measures, New Zealand focuses on the reliability of a comparable or comparables and allows the use of an arm's length range and/or statistical measures in appropriate circumstances. Where a range comprises results of relatively equal and highly reliable comparables, then any point in the range can be regarded as arm's length.

### Q11. Are comparability adjustments required under your domestic transfer pricing framework?

**Yes.**

The use of comparability adjustments is justified to the extent that comparability is improved to arrive at arm's length pricing.

## Hard-to-Value Intangibles

### Q14. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain guidance specific to hard-to-value intangibles (HTVI)?

**Yes.**

The domestic transfer pricing framework follows the guidance on HTVI in Chapter VI of the TPG.

New Zealand's domestic transfer pricing legislation explicitly refers to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines 20 22 and requires domestic rules to be applied consistently with these.

### Q15. If your jurisdiction applies the HTVI approach, what are the conditions for the application of the HTVI approach?

The conditions for the application of the HTVI are those set out in Chapter VI of the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

### Q16. Are transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach subject to a transfer pricing analysis different from the one established in Chapters I and VI?

**No.**

### Q17. What is the statute of limitations applicable to transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach in your domestic transfer pricing framework?

The statute of limitations for all transfer pricing transactions, including HTVI, is seven years after the tax year in which the relevant tax return was originally filed, if the Commissioner notifies the taxpayer that a tax audit or investigation has commenced within four years of the tax return filing. If no such notification has been provided, the statute of limitations is four years.

## Intra-group Services

### Q23. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide guidance specific to intra-group services transactions?

**Yes.**

The domestic transfer pricing framework follows the guidance in Chapter VII of the TPG.

New Zealand's domestic transfer pricing legislation explicitly refers to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines 20 22 and requires domestic rules apply consistently with these.

### Q24. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for or allow the application of a simplified approach for low value-adding intra-group services?

**Yes.**

The domestic transfer pricing framework follows the low value-adding services approach in Chapter VII.

New Zealand initially applied a threshold of $1 million for the low value-adding services simplification measure. The threshold requirement has been removed for income years commencing on or after 1 April 2021.

Sources: [Simplification measures for transfer pricing](https://www.ird.govt.nz/international-tax/business/transfer-pricing/simplification-measures)

### Q25. Are there any other rules outside your transfer pricing framework for pricing intragroup services?

### Q27. Are there any other rules outside your transfer pricing framework that are relevant for the tax treatment of financial transactions?

**Yes.**

A general summary is as follows:

1. Interest expenditure must meet the requirements of the normal deductibility rules.

2. Interest deductions are subject to the thin capitalisation regime.

3. New Zealand has implemented interest limitation rules for c ross-border related borrowing.

## Transfer Pricing Documentation

### Q29. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework require the taxpayer to prepare transfer pricing documentation?

**Yes.**

Required documentation: Master file consistent with Annex I to Chapter V of the TPG; Local file consistent with Annex II to Chapter V of the TPG; Country-by-country report consistent with Annex III to Chapter V of the TPG.

There is no explicit statutory requirement to prepare a Master file or Local file. However, New Zealand's tax system operates on a self-assessment basis, where the taxpayer is required to keep sufficient records to support its tax position. This includes transfer pricing documentation for material transfer pricing risks.

Sources: [Section 22 of the Tax Administration Act 1994 (Tax Administration Act 1994 No 166 (as at 01 April 2021), Public Act Contents – New Zealand Legislation)](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1994/0166/latest/DLM348343.html?search=qs_act%40bill%40regulation%40deemedreg_tax+administration+act_resel_25_h&p=1&sr=1)

### Q30. Please briefly explain the relevant requirements related to each transfer pricing documentation requirement.

There is no explicit statutory requirement to prepare a Master file or Local file. New Zealand's tax system operates on a self-assessment basis, where the taxpayer is required to keep sufficient records to support its tax position. This includes transfer pricing documentation for material transfer pricing risks.

### Q31. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for specific transfer pricing penalties and/or compliance incentives?

**No.**

General tax penalties may apply where an adjustment is made by Inland Revenue, normally 20% to 40% of the tax shortfall. Determination of the penalties focuses on culpability and the level of co-operation by the taxpayer.

### Q32. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for exemption from transfer pricing documentation obligations?

**N/A.**

## Dispute Avoidance and Resolution

### Q33. Which mechanisms are available in your jurisdiction to prevent and/or resolve transfer pricing disputes?

Available mechanisms: Rulings; Enhanced engagement or cooperative compliance program me s; Advance Pricing Agreements (APA): Unilateral APAs, Bilateral APAs, Multilateral APAs; Mutual Agreement Procedures.

Further information on New Zealand's APA programme can be found at Advance pricing agreements (ird.govt.nz). (Note, in New Zealand a Unilateral APA is a Ruling). Further information on New Zealand's MAP programme can be found at Mutual agreement procedure (MAP) (ird.govt.nz).

Sources: [New Zealand's MAP Profile](https://www.oecd.org/tax/dispute/New-Zealand-Dispute-Resolution-Profile.pdf)

## Safe Harbours

### Q39. Does your jurisdiction provide for any safe harbours or other simplification measures in respect of certain industries, types of taxpayers, or types of transactions?

**Yes.**

Simplification measures are available for low value-adding intra-group services in in accordance with OECD TPG; interest rates for small value loans where the loan principal does not exceed NZ$10m in total per year; and small wholesale distributors with a turnover under NZ$30m per year.

Sources: [Simplification measures for transfer pricing (ird.govt.nz)](https://www.ird.govt.nz/international-tax/business/transfer-pricing/simplification-measures)

## Other Legislative Aspects

### Q40. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow downward corresponding adjustments in the absence of a mutual agreement procedure?

**No.**

New Zealand's administrative practice is to implement downward corresponding adjustments through the mutual agreement procedure.

### Q41. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow or require taxpayers to make year-end adjustments?

**Yes.**

Year-end adjustments are allowed.

Year-end adjustments may be made to ensure that transfer prices and resulting taxable income are consistent with the arm's length principle.

Sources: [Sections GC 7 & 8 of the Income Tax Act 2007](http://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0097/latest/DLM1512301.html)

### Q42. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for secondary adjustments?

**Yes.**

A primary adjustment made by Inland Revenue may give rise to a deemed dividend and the potential application of non-resident withholding tax.

## Attribution of Profits to Permanent Establishments

### Q43. Which version of Article 7 of the OECD Model Tax Convention on Income and on Capital do your tax treaties contain?

Article 7 as it read before 2010.

Sources: [Branches](https://www.ird.govt.nz/international-tax/business/transfer-pricing/practice-issues/branches)

### Q44. For tax treaties containing Article 7 as it read before 2010, does your jurisdiction apply the authorized OECD approach?

**No.**

(please explain the approach used and which tax treaties are concerned)

New Zealand applies the commentary to Article 7 as published in the 2005 version of the OECD Model Tax Convention. New Zealand has made a specific reservation to the 2010 commentary of Article 7 and an observation in the 2008 commentary to Article 7 of the Model Tax Convention.

Sources: [Branches](https://www.ird.govt.nz/international-tax/business/transfer-pricing/practice-issues/branches)

### Q45. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain specific guidance for the attribution of profits to permanent establishments of non resident entities?

**No.**

No specific domestic guidance is provided, in practice the approach is consistent with the commentary to Article 7 as published in the 2005 version of the OECD Model Tax Convention.

Sources: [Branches](https://www.ird.govt.nz/international-tax/business/transfer-pricing/practice-issues/branches)
