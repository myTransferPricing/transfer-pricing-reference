# Switzerland transfer pricing rules

Transfer pricing rules in Switzerland, summarised from the OECD Transfer Pricing Country Profile for Switzerland (updated October 2025). The profile is the questionnaire the Switzerland tax administration completed for the OECD, covering the arm's length principle, transfer pricing methods, comparability, intangibles, intra-group services, financial transactions, documentation, dispute resolution and safe harbours. ## The Arm's Length Principle

### Q1. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework make reference to the arm's length principle?

**Yes.**

According to the case law of the Swiss Federal Supreme Court (see the specific Reference), Article 58 of the Federal Law on Federal Direct Tax of December 14, 1990 (FDTL) and Article 24 of the Federal Law on Harmonization of the Cantonal and Communal Taxes of December 14, 1990 (FTHL) create the necessary legal basis for application of the Arm's Length Principle. Both articles deal with the taxable basis for corporations.

Sources: [Article 58(1)(b or c) of the FDTL](https://www.admin.ch/opc/fr/classified-compilation/19900329/index.html); [Article 24(1)(a or b) of the FTHL](https://www.admin.ch/opc/fr/classified-compilation/19900333/index.html); [And case law (see notably):](https://www.bger.ch/ext/eurospider/live/fr/php/clir/http/index.php?lang=fr) 2C_1089/2018 of December 20, 2019, para. 11.2; ATF 140 II 88, para. 4.2.; 2C_1082 and 1083/2013 of January 14, 2015, para. 5.1; 2C_644/2013 of October 21, 2013, para. 3.1, or; 2C_834/2011 of July 6, 2012, para. 2.1

### Q2. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework give the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines any role or status?

**Yes.**

Switzerland relies on the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (OECD TPG) as a source for the interpretation of the arm's length principle.

### Q3. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for a definition of related parties applicable for transfer pricing purposes?

**Yes.**

The notion of related parties is not legally defined. However, the Swiss Federal Supreme Court has in its jurisprudence (see the specific reference) widely defined the notion of shareholder or any parties (private person or legal entity) with a commercial or a personal close relationship with the shareholder which is also relevant for transfer pricing purposes. Accordingly, an entity is considered related if, primarily, a commercial or, secondarily, a personal close relationship exists between the two entities. Therefore, direct or indirect participation in the management, control or capital is not required. The crucial question is if the tested transaction was conducted the way it was concluded only as a consequence of the associated relationship or not.

Sources: Case law: ATF 119 IB 116, para. 2

## Transfer Pricing Methods

### Q4. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for transfer pricing methods to be used in respect of transactions between related parties?

**Yes.**

Methods provided for: CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, TNMM, Profit Split, Other.

### Q5. Which criterion is provided for in your domestic transfer pricing framework for the application of transfer pricing methods?

Most appropriate method.

Switzerland relies on the methods suggested in the OECD TPG although there is no direct reference to any method in its domestic legislation. For the selection of the most appropriate method, Switzerland will typically evaluate the nature of the controlled transactions through a functions, assets and risks (FAR) analysis.

### Q6. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain specific guidance on commodity transactions?

**No.**

Swiss domestic legislation does not contain specific guidance on commodity transactions.

## Comparability Analysis

### Q7. Does your jurisdiction follow the guidance on comparability analysis outlined in Chapter III of the TPG?

**Yes.**

In practice, Switzerland relies upon the OECD TPG and follows the guidance on comparability analysis as outlined in Chapter III.

### Q8. Is there a preference in your jurisdiction for domestic comparables over foreign comparables?

**No.**

### Q9. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework permit the use of secret comparables for transfer pricing assessment purposes?

**No.**

### Q10. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow or require the use of an arm's length range and/or statistical measure for determining arm's length remuneration?

**Yes.**

In practice, statistical tools that take account of central tendency like the interquartile range or other percentiles are usually applied to narrow the range.

### Q11. Are comparability adjustments required under your domestic transfer pricing framework?

**No.**

Although comparability adjustments are not systematically required, they may be needed on a case-by-case basis if deemed appropriate.

## Intangible Property

### Q12. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain guidance specific to the pricing of controlled transactions involving intangibles?

**No.**

Swiss domestic legislation does not contain specific guidance on the pricing of controlled transactions involving intangibles and generally relies on the guidance in Chapter VI of the OECD TPG.

### Q13. Are there any other rules outside your transfer pricing framework that are relevant for the pricing of controlled transactions involving intangibles?

**No.**

## Hard-to-Value Intangibles

### Q14. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain guidance specific to hard-to-value intangibles (HTVI)?

**No.**

Swiss domestic legislation does not contain specific guidance on HTVI; however, Switzerland generally relies on the guidance contained in Chapter VI of the OECD TPG.

### Q15. If your jurisdiction applies the HTVI approach, what are the conditions for the application of the HTVI approach?

The conditions for the application of the HTVI approach are those outlined in the OECD TPG.

### Q16. Are transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach subject to a transfer pricing analysis different from the one established in Chapters I and VI?

**No.**

### Q17. What is the statute of limitations applicable to transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach in your domestic transfer pricing framework?

The statute of limitations is 10 years after the assessment, which is in line with the statute of limitations generally applicable to other transactions.

### Q18. Can taxpayers request a bilateral or multilateral advance pricing agreement for transactions falling within the scope of the HTVI approach?

**Yes.**

### Q19. What measures exist or approaches have been adopted to avoid the use of hindsight?

**None.**

### Q20. Is it possible for your tax administration to make adjustments under the HTVI approach in open years amounts pertaining to closed years?

**No.**

### Q21. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow the tax administration to make corresponding adjustments under the HTVI approach in open years for amounts pertaining to closed years?

**No.**

### Q22. Is it possible for your tax administration to make several adjustments for one single HTVI transaction under the HTVI approach?

**No.**

## Intra-group Services

### Q23. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide guidance specific to intra-group services transactions?

**Yes.**

The domestic transfer pricing framework follows the guidance in Chapter VII of the TPG.

Swiss domestic legislation contains specific guidance on intra-group services transactions in the form of a Circular Letter which specifically instructs cantonal tax administrations to rely on the OECD TPG for the remuneration of intra-group service transactions.

Additionally, the Federal Tax Administration (FTA) published on its website guidance in connection with the cost-plus method to provide clarity on its practice in line with OECD TPG.

Sources: [Cost plus method](https://www.estv.admin.ch/en/cost-plus-method)

### Q24. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for or allow the application of a simplified approach for low value-adding intra-group services?

**Yes.**

The domestic transfer pricing framework follows the low value-adding services approach in Chapter VII.

Swiss domestic legislation contains specific guidance on intra-group services transactions in the form of a Circular Letter which specifically instructs cantonal tax administrations to rely on the OECD TPG for the remuneration of intra-group service transactions.

Additionally, the FTA published on its website guidance in connection with the cost-plus method to provide clarity on its practice in line with OECD TPG.

Sources: [Cost plus method](https://www.estv.admin.ch/en/cost-plus-method)

### Q25. Are there any other rules outside your transfer pricing framework for pricing intragroup services?

**No.**

## Financial Transactions

### Q26. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide guidance specific to financial transactions?

**Yes.**

The domestic transfer pricing framework follows the guidance in Chapter X of the TPG.

Swiss domestic legislation contains certain specific guidance on financial transactions in the form of Circular Letters published annually by the FTA on the interest rate recognized for tax purposes for advances or loans in Swiss francs or foreign currencies. Additionally, the FTA published a circular concerning hidden capital contribution in corporations and cooperatives.

In general, Swiss domestic legislation relies on the guidance in Chapter X of the OECD TPG in relation to financial transactions.

The FTA also published on its website guidance in connection with financial transactions to provide clarity on its practice.

Sources: [Intra-group loans](https://www.estv.admin.ch/en/intra-group-loans)

### Q27. Are there any other rules outside your transfer pricing framework that are relevant for the tax treatment of financial transactions?

**No.**

The FTA publishes annually circular letters on the interest rate recognized for tax purposes for advances or loans in Swiss francs or foreign currencies. Additionally, the FTA published a circular concerning hidden capital contribution in corporations and cooperatives.

This administrative guidance represents a "safe harbour" rule. Taxpayers can deviate from the safe harbour rules by providing the tax authority a transfer pricing study.

## Cost Contribution Arrangements

### Q28. Does your jurisdiction allow cost contribution arrangements?

**No.**

Swiss domestic legislation does not contain specific guidance on cost contribution arrangements and generally relies on the guidance in Chapter VIII of the OECD TPG.

## Transfer Pricing Documentation

### Q29. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework require the taxpayer to prepare transfer pricing documentation?

**Yes.**

Documentation required: Country-by-country report consistent with Annex III to Chapter V of the TPG.

Besides the obligation to file a Country-by-country report, there is no additional specific requirement concerning transfer pricing documentation.

Sources: [Federal Act on the International Automatic Exchange of Country-by-Country Reports of Multinational Enterprise Groups (CbCR law; entry into force 1st December 2017)](https://www.admin.ch/opc/fr/federal-gazette/2017/3977.pdf); [Ordinance on the International Automatic Exchange of Country-by-Country Reports of Multinationals (CbCR ordinance; entry into force 1st December 2017)](https://www.newsd.admin.ch/newsd/message/attachments/49802.pdf)

### Q30. Please briefly explain the relevant requirements related to each transfer pricing documentation requirement.

Swiss domestic legislation requires the taxpayer to provide all documents necessary for properly assessing the taxable income. In the case of related transactions, the taxpayer has to demonstrate that the transfer prices used are based on the arm's length principle. Therefore, the preparation of transfer pricing documentation to document intra-group transactions is considered best practice and has become a standard requirement.

In the case of MAPs and APAs, Master file, Local file and all relevant information for the resolution of the case are usually required.

Sources: [Article 124 FDTL](https://www.admin.ch/opc/fr/classified-compilation/19900329/index.html); [Article 42 FTHL](https://www.admin.ch/opc/fr/classified-compilation/19900333/index.html)

### Q31. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for specific transfer pricing penalties and/or compliance incentives?

**Yes.**

The Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) law contains notification mechanisms that will apply to Ultimate Parent Entities and Surrogate Parent Entities in Switzerland. Furthermore, there are also penalties in relation to the filing and registration obligations of CbCR: (i) penalties for non-filing or late filing (ii) penalties for incorrect or inaccurate filing, and (iii) general penalties for non-compliance with the FTA's orders. Switzerland may also conduct inspections to verify that the obligations of the Constituent Entities are fulfilled.

Besides the obligation to file a Country-by-country report, Switzerland does not have specific transfer pricing legal documentation requirements, but if requested by the FTA or the cantonal tax authorities, taxpayers must demonstrate that the transfer prices applied rely on sound economic and commercial reasoning and comply with the arm's length principle. Sanctions may apply if the taxpayer does not collaborate with the tax authorities.

Sources: [Federal Act on the International Automatic Exchange of Country-by-Country Reports of Multinationals (CbCR law; entry into force 1st December 2017)](https://www.admin.ch/opc/fr/federal-gazette/2017/3977.pdf); Circular Letter of the Federal Tax Administration of 4 March 1997 (not available online)

### Q32. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for exemption from transfer pricing documentation obligations?

**Yes.**

The CbCR law provides for exemptions only consistent with Annex III to Chapter V of the TPG (e.g., for MNEs with total consolidated group revenue of 900 million Swiss Francs or less [see Article 6 CbCR law and Article 2 CbCR ordinance]).

Sources: [Federal Act on the International Automatic Exchange of Country-by-Country Reports of Multinationals (CbCR law; entry into force 1st December 2017)](https://www.admin.ch/opc/fr/federal-gazette/2017/3977.pdf); [Ordinance on the International Automatic Exchange of Country-by-Country Reports of Multinationals (CbCR ordinance; entry into force 1st December 2017)](https://www.newsd.admin.ch/newsd/message/attachments/49802.pdf)

## Dispute Avoidance and Resolution

### Q33. Which mechanisms are available in your jurisdiction to prevent and/or resolve transfer pricing disputes?

Available mechanisms: Rulings; Advance Pricing Agreements (APA): Unilateral APAs, Bilateral APAs, Multilateral APAs; Mutual Agreement Procedures.

Switzerland does not have a formal APA programme in place, but it is authorised to enter bilateral or multilateral APAs on the basis of the MAP provision in the applicable tax treaty. It allows for roll-back of APAs if consistent with Switzerland's domestic time limit of 10 years. Usually, Switzerland seeks to agree to APAs over a period of 5 years, but it can vary in practice.

Moreover, Switzerland has a long-standing experience in the resolution of MAPs. The relevant details can be found in Switzerland's MAP profile and the webpage of the State Secretariat for International Finance.

Sources: [Switzerland MAP profile](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/dispute-resolution/switzerland-dispute-resolution-profile.pdf); [Factsheet on the mutual agreement procedure](https://backend.sif.admin.ch/fileservice/sdweb-docs-prod-sifadminch-files/files/2024/12/19/399fcd63-ed39-4d95-9f91-2c406e132c8f.pdf)

## Safe Harbours

### Q39. Does your jurisdiction provide for any safe harbours or other simplification measures in respect of certain industries, types of taxpayers, or types of transactions?

**Yes.**

The FTA has issued circulars containing safe harbours rules concerning thin capitalization and intra-group interest rate.

The financial statements of a company can be changed, among other things to make year-end adjustments, until they are formally approved by the competent organ.

Sources: [Circulaire no 6a of the Federal Tax Administration of 10 October 2004 on hidden capital](https://www.estv2.admin.ch/dvs/kreisschreiben/dbst-ks-2024-006a-dvs-fr.pdf); [Taux d'intérêt de l'impôt anticipé - updated regularly and published on the website of the Federal Tax Administration](https://www.estv.admin.ch/fr/taux-interet-impot-anticipe)

## Other Legislative Aspects

### Q40. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow downward corresponding adjustments in the absence of a mutual agreement procedure?

**Yes.**

Downward corresponding adjustments in the absence of a mutual agreement procedure may be possible under Swiss domestic law in certain circumstances, on the initiative of the competent cantonal tax authority, and provided that the primary adjustment made by the foreign tax authority complies with the arm's length principle.

### Q41. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework allow or require taxpayers to make year-end adjustments?

**Yes.**

Year-end adjustments are allowed.

### Q42. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework provide for secondary adjustments?

**Yes.**

In Switzerland, a secondary adjustment represents the levying of withholding tax on the amount that qualifies as a hidden profit distribution in the context of transfer pricing. Secondary adjustments are carried out exclusively by the Swiss FTA, which has sole authority for levying such withholding tax, and may be done independently of a primary adjustment by the competent cantonal tax authority.

## Attribution of Profits to Permanent Establishments

### Q43. Which version of Article 7 of the OECD Model Tax Convention on Income and on Capital do your tax treaties contain?

Article 7 as it read before 2010 and Article 7 as it reads after 2010.

Switzerland has about 100 DTA, some of them concluded many years ago. The content of Article 7 in a given DTA represents the result of bilateral negotiation.

### Q44. For tax treaties containing Article 7 as it read before 2010, does your jurisdiction apply the authorized OECD approach?

**Yes.**

### Q45. Does your domestic transfer pricing framework contain specific guidance for the attribution of profits to permanent establishments of non resident entities?

**No.**

See answer to question 44.
